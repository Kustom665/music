Editor's Pick

Midnight Static — Lower Frequencies

9.1

A record that sounds like it was mixed at 3am and meant to be heard that way. "Lower Frequencies" trades the band's earlier pop instincts for something murkier, slower, and far more interesting — proof that restraint can hit harder than a chorus.

The opening run of "Static Hymn" and "Basement Weather" sets the terms: drums mixed back, guitars smeared into the room, vocals close enough to catch the breath between lines. Nothing here chases a single. The nearest thing to one, "Porch Light, Off", is the quietest song on the record and the best.

It sags once. The eight-minute "Interference" circles a good idea for about two minutes too long. But the closing pair recovers everything, and the last forty seconds of "Lower Frequencies (Reprise)" are the most affecting thing the band has recorded.

Verdict: a confident turn toward the dark that rewards headphones, a late night, and patience.
